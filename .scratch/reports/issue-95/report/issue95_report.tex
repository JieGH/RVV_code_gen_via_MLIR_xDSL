\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Why our YOLO GEMM trails H\'ector's at 1 thread}\\[4pt]
The order in which our packer reads A\\[2pt]
Issue \iss{95} (follows \iss{94}) \quad$\cdot$\quad 18 Nov 2026
\end{center}

\section*{Answer}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{Main cause: our A packer reads A in a slow order.} A is stored column by column. Our packer fills one 24-row panel at a time and, for each panel, reads 24 floats from every column, jumping a full column (19.2 KB on Y10) between reads. H\'ector's packer reads each column once from top to bottom and spreads it over the panels. Same bytes, same packed result.
\item \textbf{The pack takes most of our time} on the large-m shapes: 77\% on Y10 (4800$\times$16$\times$32) and 90\% on Y05 (19200$\times$8$\times$144) (instrumented build, shares only). Our compute part on Y10 runs at about 7.8 GOPs, faster than H\'ector's whole call (5.1).
\item \textbf{The gap sits where A is large and packed in one block of k.} The 23 shapes with m $>$ 300 and k $\le$ kc carry 131.0 ms of the 140.5 ms 1-thread gap on the m $>$ 300 shapes. On them the pack test puts the order cost at 162.2 ms per pass: the same size as the gap. On the 10 shapes with k $>$ kc the gap is only 9.5 ms. On the 17 shapes with m = 300, A is small and we are 27.0 ms ahead.
\item \textbf{The pack test overstates absolute pack times} (on \input{\D/over.tex} it takes longer than our whole GEMM call), most on k $>$ kc. Its numbers show the size of the order effect, not an exact share of our time. Only an in-GEMM change can give the exact gain.
\item \textbf{Not the main cause:} the block size along m (forcing mc to 240 lifts Y10 by about 25\%, not to his level), and our 24x8 microkernel.
\item \textbf{No fix in this issue.} The fix (a column-order A packer) is the pending issue \iss{96}.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Glossary}
\begin{description}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}\small
\item[ours, H\'ector's] our GEMM (xDSL Project Generator, generated RVV microkernel); H\'ector's \texttt{gemm.bin} from GEMMLib\_yolo at \texttt{b292c92}, his code unchanged.
\item[shape, Y\textit{nn}] one YOLO26 layer as an m $\times$ n $\times$ k GEMM (\texttt{datasets/yolo26\_k1.dat}).
\item[A, column-major, lda] A is m $\times$ k, stored column by column; one column is m contiguous floats, so the next column starts lda = m floats later.
\item[pack A] copy a block of A (mc rows $\times$ kc columns) into panels of MR rows, so the microkernel reads it contiguously. Done once per block, inside the timed call.
\item[panel-outer (our order)] for each MR-row panel, for each of the kc columns: read MR floats.
\item[column-outer (his order)] for each of the kc columns, for each MR-row panel: read MR floats.
\item[mc, nc, kc] block sizes: rows of A, columns of B, depth. Ours from the analytical cache model per shape; H\'ector's fixed (MC = KC = 256, NC = n).
\item[tile MRxNR] the microkernel's block of C. Ours 24x8; H\'ector's 16x8 (16x4 at n = 4).
\item[1T] 1 thread, pinned to core 4.
\item[uninstrumented build] \texttt{INSTRUMENTED=0}, no timers inside the GEMM. Every GOPs value here comes from it.
\item[instrumented build] timers around pack and compute (\texttt{--instr}, warm-up protocol). The timers cost 11--17\%, so only its times as shares and its counters are used.
\item[reference protocol] no warm-up; call the GEMM until \texttt{tmin} seconds have passed; mean time per call. H\'ector's driver uses the same loop (\texttt{TIMIN}).
\item[GOPs by mean time / by median rep] 2mnk / mean time per call; 2mnk / median call time (ours only, his driver keeps no per-call time).
\item[probe run] one shape, ours then H\'ector's, back to back (\texttt{probe\_issue95.sh}, \texttt{--tmin 1}).
\item[pack test] \texttt{issue95/pack\_order.c}: packs the same A both ways on the board and times each.
\item[order cost] pack time in our order minus pack time in his order, same blocks.
\item[gap] our time minus H\'ector's time per call, 1T, from \iss{94} (mean of 3 launches each).
\end{description}

\section{Where the gap is (from \iss{94})}
At 1T, \iss{94} measured our pass at 696.9 ms (4.27 GOPs) and H\'ector's at 583.4 ms (5.10 GOPs). Per shape, we lose most on large m with small k (Y10 4800$\times$16$\times$32: 0.31 of his GOPs; Y00, Y01, Y03 0.39--0.43) and win every shape with m = 300 (1.03--1.73). Y10 became the test shape: largest loss, 4.9 MFLOP per call.

\section{Method}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Test loop.} \texttt{probe\_issue95.sh M N K} runs one shape on both sides, back to back, and refuses to start while another user is busy on the board. Red when ours $<$ 0.8 of his.
\item \textbf{Code read} of both loop nests (\texttt{gemm\_dispatch\_xdsl.cpp}; his \texttt{gemm\_B3A2C0} in \texttt{gemm\_blis.c}). Both are BLIS loops in the same order (jc, pc: pack B, ic: pack A, jr, ir, microkernel). His build has \texttt{-DGEMMLIB\_ON} and no \texttt{OPENBLAS\_ON}/\texttt{BLIS\_ON}, so \texttt{gemm.bin} times his own code.
\item \textbf{Hypotheses, ranked before testing:} H1 mc too large; H2 our microkernel slower; H3 our A pack costs more; H4 compiler flags.
\item \textbf{Probes}, one change at a time: mc forced (\texttt{RVV\_FORCE\_MC}, a new opt-in knob, off by default), the instrumented split, and the pack test.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Not measured here}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Our GEMM with a column-order A packer. This changes the generated packer (the vector path); it is the pending issue \iss{96}.
\item 8 threads. Each thread packs its own A block the same way; the 8T gap also includes H\'ector's Y00 behaviour (\iss{94}).
\item MR 16 against MR 24 in his order (0.34 against 0.71 ms for Y10's A in two separate pack tests): a smaller, second effect.
\item H4 (compiler). Our kernels already build at \texttt{-O3}; only the driver and pack C++ build at \texttt{-O2}.
\end{itemize}
