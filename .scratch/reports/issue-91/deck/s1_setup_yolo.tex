\begin{frame}{Three questions}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{8pt}
\item \textbf{H\'ector (YOLO26):} which microkernel sizes (MR$\times$NR) run fastest for the YOLO GEMM shapes, on 1 thread and in the parallel GEMM with 8 threads? (\iss{58})
\item \textbf{Paper shapes (BERT B1--B5, Square S1--S5):} which tile wins at 1 and 8 threads, and do the paper's picks still hold? (\iss{71})
\item \textbf{Packing:} does the vector packer beat a scalar packer, and which of m, n, k, tile and thread count decide it? (\iss{83}, \iss{85})
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{6pt}
Every number comes from a committed log. Design: this repo's xDSL Project Generator (BLIS-style FP32 GEMM, generated RVV microkernel).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Glossary}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{3.0cm} X@{}}
\toprule
m $\times$ n $\times$ k & C (m $\times$ n) += A (m $\times$ k) $\cdot$ B (k $\times$ n). For YOLO and BERT, each packed element of A is reused n times. \\
tile MR$\times$NR & The microkernel's block of C: MR rows by NR columns. 19 Candidate Tiles per shape in the sweeps. \\
best tile & For one shape (or one n group), the tile with the highest GOPs by mean time, averaged over launches. \\
GOPs by mean time & 2mnk $\div$ mean time per call. Main metric. \\
GOPs by median rep & Median over reps of each rep's GOPs. Less sensitive to a few slow reps. \\
launch & One full run of the test (every tile on every shape). Written ``launch 1/2/3''. \\
A-B-B-A & Run order that interleaves two sides (e.g.\ ours, his, his, ours), so slow drift hits both. \\
launch range & Lowest to highest launch. A gap inside it is not called a difference (marked $\sim$ or open dot). \\
packer & Copies a block of A or B into a contiguous buffer before the microkernel reads it. Vector: RVV loads/stores. Scalar (\texttt{--scalar-pack}): one float per load/store. Same layout. \\
pack share & Pack time $\div$ (pack + compute), from the instrumented build. \\
extra pack cost & Scalar pack time $\div$ vector pack time. \\
s/v & Scalar GOPs $\div$ vector GOPs. Below 1: vector packing is faster. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Correctness}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{6pt}
\item \textbf{Parallel engine (\iss{56}):} tile 24x8, 50 YOLO shapes, 1T and 8T: 50 of 50 PASS against the scalar oracle in all 6 launches.
\item \textbf{8-thread tile sweep (\iss{63}):} the timing launches ran with the oracle off. The audit (\iss{79} C3) checked all 19 tiles at 8 threads on all 50 shapes: \textbf{950 of 950 PASS} (\texttt{logs/run\_2026-11-17\_092354}, jbpi).
\item \textbf{Host tests:} 21/21 \texttt{test\_parallel\_gemm.py}; 1-thread generated code byte-identical with the feature off.
\item \textbf{Scalar packer (\iss{83}):} C1--C4, oracle on: YOLO-like 76/76 (1T, 8T), paper-like 57/57 (1T, 8T).
\item \textbf{Sweep points (\iss{85}):} 30/30 PASS per packer on each board.
\item H\'ector's \texttt{gemm.bin}: his own check printed OK on every row (\iss{62}).
\end{itemize}
