\section{Part 1: H\'ector's YOLO request}

\subsection{The request and what was built}
H\'ector, 13 Nov 2026: \emph{``sería interesante que observaras los tamaños de micro-kernels que mejor rinden para la versión paralela de la GEMM con 8 hilos''} (look at which microkernel sizes run fastest in the parallel GEMM with 8 threads). Adri\'an asked whether a size other than H\'ector's 16x8 / 16x4 would help. H\'ector's goal is whole YOLO inference under 0.5 s, multi-threaded; an fp16 version with MR = 32 is planned.

\midrule

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item The best tile changes with threads: MR = 24 wins at 1T; at 8T, MR = 32 for n $\le$ 64 and MR = 16 with wide NR (14--15) for n $\ge$ 80.
\item \textbf{Read the 8T list as a family} (\iss{79} B4): only n = 4, 8 and 32 keep the same winner in all 3 launches; for the others the gap to the runner-up is smaller than the launch range. What holds: MR 32 / NR 4 for n $\le$ 8, MR 32 / NR 6--7 for n = 16--64, MR 16 / NR 12--15 for n $\ge$ 80.
\item The single-tile ranking is tight: 7 tiles within 145.6--149.6 ms. Picking the tile per shape saves about 8\% over the best single tile (134.1 against 145.6 ms).
\item \textbf{Picked on one launch, scored on the other two}, the per-shape choice is still 8--11 ms per pass faster than the best single tile (\iss{79} B3).
\item \textbf{Mean time against median rep at 8T:} on mid-size shapes our median rep is far above our mean (Y32: 61.09 against 38.19 GOPs; Y34 at 16x15: 60.00 against 37.33). A few reps per launch are much slower; at 1T the two metrics agree within about 0.1\%. Likely cause: core 0 takes every device interrupt (13.1\% system time against 0.2--0.5\% on cores 1--7, \iss{79} B6); not tested.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{His Y00 decides the totals.} It took 12.9, 62.6 and 105.5 ms in his three launches here (6.7--105.5 ms over all 9 of his launches); ours is steady at 10.6 GOPs (32x4). Without Y00 his passes are steady (122.1--124.2 ms).
\item \textbf{Tile for tile, his engine is about 15\% faster} (123.4 against 141.6 ms, without Y00). Choosing the tile per shape recovers most of the gap, not all: 127.9 against his 123.4 ms (3.7\% behind).
\item His 8T pass here (183.7 ms) differs from his separate \iss{62} run (135.7 ms); the difference is mostly Y00.
\item By median rep our best tile beats his mean on 39 of 50 shapes (25 of 50 by mean time); his driver has no median, so this compares different metrics and is not used for the verdict.
\item Why his Y00 swings is not measured: 300 barriers per call, or Y00 being the first call of each process (\iss{82} \S2).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Correctness}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \iss{56}: tile 24x8, 50 YOLO shapes, 1T and 8T: 50 of 50 PASS against the scalar oracle in all 6 launches.
\item \iss{63}: the timing launches ran with the oracle off. The audit (\iss{79} C3) checked all 19 tiles at 8 threads on all 50 shapes: 950 of 950 PASS (\texttt{logs/run\_2026-11-17\_092354}, jbpi).
\item Host tests: 21 of 21 in \texttt{test\_parallel\_gemm.py}, 623 of 623 in the full suite (\iss{79} C1); with the features off the generated code is byte-identical to before.
\item H\'ector's \texttt{gemm.bin} printed OK on every row of every launch (\iss{62}).
\end{itemize}
