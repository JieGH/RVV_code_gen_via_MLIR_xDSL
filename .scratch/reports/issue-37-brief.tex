\title{\vspace{-1.2cm}\textbf{32$\times$6 vs 32$\times$7, settled inside one launch}\\[2pt]
\large Brief of issue \iss{37} (settled, decision: keep 32$\times$7), from the \iss{31}--\iss{34} audit}
\author{RVV\_code\_gen\_via\_MLIR\_xDSL, \texttt{pure-xdsl}\\ commit \texttt{d810e5b}}
\date{28 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-0.8cm}

\paragraph{Scope.} \iss{32} read 32$\times$6 ahead of the shipped 32$\times$7 in 8 of 8 pairs (0.5--3.3\%), but
every one of those pairs ran on core 0 --- later shown (\iss{39} audit) to run 5--10\% slower than a pinned
non-zero core, and itself not a controlled comparison (each tile its own launch, not two rows of one launch).
\iss{37} asked whether the gap survives a fair test: both tiles inside \textbf{one} launch, on a non-zero core,
repeated over several launches, deciding only if one tile wins in every launch (the \iss{39} house rules).
\textbf{Every speed number here is from the uninstrumented build} (\texttt{INSTRUMENTED=0}), reference protocol,
\texttt{--tmin 10}, core 4, board \texttt{jbpi2}. Speed is in GOPs, by mean time (the reference's own formula)
and by median rep --- the two matched to within 0.01 GOPs in every case below. Every log cited is committed
(Appendix~A).

\paragraph{Cases} (config \texttt{config\_issue37\_nr6\_vs\_nr7.json}, \texttt{--no-nc-overrides}):
B4 = 4096$\times$384$\times$1024; S1--S5 = the square shapes, 1000$^3$ up to 5000$^3$. Both tiles run against
identical shapes (\texttt{issue37\_b4\_square\_32x6.dat} mirrors the shipped \texttt{issue2\_square\_bert\_32x7.dat}),
each its own row/\texttt{pj\_gen/} project, built and run back to back within one launch.

\section{Bottom line}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \good{32$\times$7 wins all 6 cases in all 3 launches --- 18 of 18.} No case and no launch went the other
  way; the earlier \iss{32} result does not survive a controlled, non-zero-core test.
  \item The margin is not close: \up{3.7--8.7\%} on the 3-launch average, above the \iss{39} noise threshold
  ($\sim$3\%) that would otherwise call for more launches. B4 has the smallest gap (3.7\%); S2 the largest (8.7\%).
  \item \textbf{Decision: no change.} 32$\times$7 stays pinned for B4 and S1--S5 in \texttt{config.json}. The
  comparison config and dataset are kept in the repo for anyone who wants to rerun it.
  \item Board checked for other users' processes before each of the 3 launches; clear every time, so nothing here
  is contention noise.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{How to read it.} 32$\times$7 is ahead in all 18 cells; there is no launch and no shape where 32$\times$6
catches up. S3--S5 at 32$\times$6 fit only 1--2 reps in \texttt{tmin}=10s in launch 1 (flagged as near-single cold
samples in that run's log); the same shapes got more reps in launches 2--3 and the ranking did not move, so the
thin-rep launch-1 points do not change the decision.
