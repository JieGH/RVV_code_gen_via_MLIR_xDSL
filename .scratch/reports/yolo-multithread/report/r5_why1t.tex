\section{Part 5: why ours trails H\'ector's at 1 thread (\iss{95})}\label{sec:95}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{Main cause: our A packer reads A in a slow order.} A is stored column by column. Our packer fills one 24-row panel at a time and, for each panel, reads 24 floats from every column, jumping a full column (19.2 KB on Y10) between reads. H\'ector's packer reads each column once from top to bottom and spreads it over the panels. Same bytes, same packed result.
\item \textbf{The pack takes most of our time} on the large-m shapes: 77\% on Y10 (4800$\times$16$\times$32) and 90\% on Y05 (19200$\times$8$\times$144) (instrumented build, shares only). Our compute part on Y10 runs at about 7.8 GOPs, faster than H\'ector's whole call (5.1).
\item \textbf{The gap sits where A is large and packed in one block of k:} 131.0 of the 140.5 ms 1T gap on the m $>$ 300 shapes is on the 23 shapes with k $\le$ kc, where the pack test puts the order cost at 162.2 ms per pass. On the 17 shapes with m = 300 we are 27.0 ms ahead.
\item \textbf{The pack test overstates absolute pack times}, most on k $>$ kc; it shows the size of the order effect, not an exact share. Only an in-GEMM change can give the exact gain.
\item \textbf{Not the main cause:} the block size along m, and our 24x8 microkernel. \textbf{No fix here:} the column-order A packer is the pending issue \iss{96}.
\end{itemize}
Terms: see the glossary (panel-outer / column-outer, pack test, order cost / gap, probe run).

\subsection{Where the gap is (from \iss{94})}
At 1T, \iss{94} measured our pass at 697.2 ms (4.27 GOPs) and H\'ector's at 583.4 ms (5.10 GOPs). Per shape, we lose most on large m with small k (Y10 4800$\times$16$\times$32: 0.31 of his GOPs; Y00, Y01, Y03 0.39--0.43) and win 16 of the 17 shapes with m = 300 (1.03--1.73; Y47 300$\times$4$\times$16 is the exception at 0.89, slower in all 3 launches). Y10 became the test shape: largest loss, 4.9 MFLOP per call.

\subsection{Method}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Test loop.} \texttt{probe\_issue95.sh M N K} runs one shape on both sides, back to back, and refuses to start while another user is busy on the board. Red when ours $<$ 0.8 of his.
\item \textbf{Code read} of both loop nests (\texttt{gemm\_dispatch\_xdsl.cpp}; his \texttt{gemm\_B3A2C0} in \texttt{gemm\_blis.c}). Both are BLIS loops in the same order (jc, pc: pack B, ic: pack A, jr, ir, microkernel). His build has \texttt{-DGEMMLIB\_ON} and no \texttt{OPENBLAS\_ON}/\texttt{BLIS\_ON}, so \texttt{gemm.bin} times his own code.
\item \textbf{Hypotheses, ranked before testing:} H1 mc too large; H2 our microkernel slower; H3 our A pack costs more; H4 compiler flags.
\item \textbf{Probes}, one change at a time: mc forced (\texttt{RVV\_FORCE\_MC}, a new opt-in knob, off by default), the instrumented split, and the pack test.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Not measured here}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Our GEMM with a column-order A packer. This changes the generated packer (the vector path); it is the pending issue \iss{96}.
\item 8 threads. Each thread packs its own A block the same way; the 8T gap also includes H\'ector's Y00 behaviour (\iss{94}).
\item MR 16 against MR 24 in his order (0.34 against 0.71 ms for Y10's A in two separate pack tests): a smaller, second effect.
\item H4 (compiler). Our kernels already build at \texttt{-O3}; only the driver and pack C++ build at \texttt{-O2}.
\end{itemize}
