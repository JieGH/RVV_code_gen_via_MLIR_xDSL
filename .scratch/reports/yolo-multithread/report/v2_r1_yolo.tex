\subsection{The best single tile for YOLO (\iss{109})}\label{sec:best}
\textbf{Rule (user, 18 Nov 2026):} one tile for all layers at a given thread count; no tile per layer. H\'ector's code runs unchanged at his own tiles. The best single tile is the tile with the shortest \emph{pass}: per tile, take each layer's mean time per call, average it over the launches, and add it up over the layers (each layer once; how often each layer runs per image is not known, \iss{58}). Only our own tiles in our own GEMM take part; H\'ector's code plays no part in the pick.

\paragraph{Why a re-search.} \iss{61} (1T) and \iss{63} (8T) timed our 19 tiles on the 50 layers with n $>$ 1, 3 launches each. At 1T, 24x8 won every launch. At 8T, 16x12 won the mean by 0.2 ms over 32x7 and only 1 of 3 launches; seven tiles lay within 2.8\%, inside the noise (\iss{39}). So \iss{109} re-timed the leading tiles, ranked over the 50 layers with n $>$ 1: 1T, the top 4 of \iss{61}, 4 launches; 8T, the top 7 of \iss{63} plus 24x8, 6 launches; tile order rotated per launch, every row checked by the oracle.

\paragraph{The re-search.} Both runs on 19 Nov 2026, jbpi, idle board; every launch finished and every row of every launch matched the oracle.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{1T: 24x8.} 697.5 ms per pass over the 50 layers with n $>$ 1 (launch range 695.4--700.0), fastest in all 4 launches. The runner-up 24x9 (708.0, range 705.7--709.2) does not overlap it. The \iss{61} pick holds.
\item \textbf{8T: 32x7, not 16x12.} 32x7 140.6 ms (range 139.8--141.3), fastest in 5 of 6 launches; 16x12 141.5 ms (range 140.3--142.7), fastest in 1. The ranges overlap, so by the rule the two tie and the mean winner, 32x7, is used. The leading 7 tiles lie within 2.1\% (140.6--143.6 ms). 24x8, the 8T tile of \iss{56} and \iss{94}, is 156.1 ms, about 11\% slower than 32x7.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Correctness}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \iss{56}: tile 24x8, 50 YOLO shapes, 1T and 8T: 50 of 50 PASS against the scalar oracle in all 6 launches.
\item \iss{94}: tile 24x8 with the scalar packer, oracle on, small YOLO-like set: C1 (1T) and C2 (8T) 4 of 4 PASS; every timed launch of V and S 50 of 50 PASS (12 launches); H\'ector's 53 of 53 OK in each of his 6 launches.
\item \iss{109}: C1 (24x8, 1T) and C2 (32x7, 8T), scalar packer, oracle on, small YOLO-like set plus the 3 n = 1 rows (our GEMM still computes them correctly): 7 of 7 PASS each; every timed launch of V and S 53 of 53 PASS (12 launches); H\'ector's 53 of 53 OK in each of his 6 launches. The re-search: every row PASS in all 10 launches.
\item \iss{63}: the timing launches ran with the oracle off. The audit (\iss{79} C3) checked all 19 tiles at 8 threads on all 50 shapes: 950 of 950 PASS (\texttt{logs/run\_2026-11-17\_092354}, jbpi).
\item Host tests: 21 of 21 in \texttt{test\_parallel\_gemm.py}, 623 of 623 in the full suite (\iss{79} C1); with the features off the generated code is byte-identical to before.
\item H\'ector's \texttt{gemm.bin} printed OK on every row of every launch (\iss{62}).
\end{itemize}
