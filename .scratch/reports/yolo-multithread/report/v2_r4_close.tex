\section{Part 4: what to tell H\'ector}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{1 thread:} in our GEMM, 24x8 gives 12\% more GOPs than H\'ector's tile over the 50 shapes (698.9 against 785.3 ms per pass, \iss{61}). H\'ector's own engine at 1T is faster than ours (586.8 against 702.8 ms, 1.20$\times$, same series, \iss{109}), and 24x8 is not in H\'ector's library.
\item \textbf{Whole engine at 8T (\iss{109}, same settings both sides, ours at 32x7):} ours 1.11$\times$ faster over the 50 layers with n $>$ 1 (21.14 against 19.00 GOPs); without Y00 H\'ector's leads by 1.04$\times$ (22.78 against 21.81). With thread binding his Y00 is steady at about 29 ms, ours about 7 ms; without binding it swings (6.7--105.5 ms, \S\ref{sec:63}). At 24x8 (\iss{94}) the two were level: the 8T tile matters.
\item \textbf{8 threads}, in our GEMM: pick the tile by n (family as in the Answer). In the \iss{63} sweep, against H\'ector's tile (149.8 ms per pass): 32x7 is 3\% faster (145.9 ms), best tile per shape 10\% faster (134.2 ms).
\item \textbf{Caveat:} the tiles were timed in \emph{our} parallel GEMM. H\'ector's GEMM has other blocking (MC = KC = 256), thread split and kernels (fp32 32x8, 16x16, 8x16, 16x8, 16x4, 8x8; no NR 4/6/7 kernel at MR 32). fp16 was not tested.
\item \textbf{Keep a vector packer} (measured on ours against a scalar packer, not on H\'ector's im2col + pack path): with a scalar packer YOLO keeps 0.929 / 0.945 of the GOPs (1T / 8T), 0.58 at n = 4.
\item \textbf{Ask H\'ector:} did H\'ector mean ``best tiles in a parallel GEMM'' or ``best tiles for \emph{H\'ector's} GEMM''? And the number of calls per shape per image, to weight the shapes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Our 1T gap (\iss{95})}
The cause is found (Part 5, \S\ref{sec:95}); the fix, packing A column by column, is pending in \iss{96}, not started. Its exact gain in our GEMM is not measured.

\subsection*{One essential test, not yet run}
\textbf{Our tile family inside H\'ector's GEMM} (\iss{82} \S1, \iss{79} part D). H\'ector's \texttt{gemm\_B3A2C0} (GEMMLib\_yolo \texttt{b292c92}) in a scratch copy, choosing by n among kernels H\'ector's library already has (32x8, 16x16; no new kernel code), timed against H\'ector's stock build: A-B-B-A (stock, patched, patched, stock), 8 threads, idle jbpi, \texttt{TIMIN=2.0}. \subsection*{Not run; stated as limits}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{H\'ector's Y00 swing} (6.7--105.5 ms over 9 launches): ours against H\'ector's is quoted with and without Y00 (\iss{82} \S2). With 8T binding it is steady but slow: 29.5--30.4 ms (\iss{94}), 28.6--29.1 ms (\iss{109}). Why is not measured.
\item \textbf{Our slow reps at 8T}: likely core 0, which takes the interrupts. They lower GOPs by mean time on mid-size shapes; they do not change the tile family (\iss{82} \S3).
\item \textbf{BERT/Square packer comparison} crosses boards and days (vector jbpi, scalar jbpi2). \iss{85} shows on one board that large n gives no packer difference at 8T.
\item \textbf{Two \iss{85} loss cases} (1T compute after vector packing; 8T 16x4 uninstrumented only): open, neither from the pack.
\item \textbf{\iss{85} crosses boards} for 8T against 1T (1T jbpi, 8T jbpi2); its shapes are synthetic, around one base point; it compares two packers, not packing against no packing.
\item \textbf{Pass times are unweighted} sums over the distinct layers (50, n $>$ 1): calls per image are unknown (\iss{58} \S5.4). H\'ector's fast path packs A inside im2col; ours always packs A, so our times include an A pack H\'ector's pre-packed path skips (\iss{58} \S5.3).
\item \textbf{Tile list:} every sweep uses the 19-tile YOLO list, not the paper's full heatmap; the \iss{109} re-search timed only the leaders of that list.
\item \textbf{Paper shapes:} this repo's design, not the paper's code; the paper's GFLOPS are shown, not compared.
\item \textbf{fp16} out of scope. \textbf{n = 1 (GEMV):} left out of every result; our design has no GEMV.
\end{itemize}
