\date{27 September 2026}

\paragraph{Scope.} B3 (64$\times$384$\times$384) has two speeds: in most reps it drops into a slow state where the
L1D prefetcher does about half its work (\iss{36}, \iss{41}, \iss{42}). \iss{43} tried one fix. On the X60 a store to
a line that is not in the cache was expected to read the line from the L2 first, even when the packer then overwrites
all of it. \texttt{cbo.zero} puts a zero line in the cache without that read. This brief covers what was built, how it
was checked and reviewed, and 24 board launches: 12 on \texttt{jbpi} and 12 on \texttt{jbpi2}, all on core 4, switch
off/on in the order A-B-B-A-A-B. \textbf{Speed numbers come from the uninstrumented build} (\texttt{INSTRUMENTED=0},
reference protocol, \texttt{--tmin 10}). \textbf{Counts come from the instrumented build} (\texttt{INSTRUMENTED=1},
warm-up protocol, 1000 reps, \texttt{--raw-events x60-l2}); they are not speed numbers to quote. Every log cited is
committed (Appendix~A).

\section{Bottom line}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Built:} \texttt{--zero-pack-lines}, off by default. Before each pack it clears every whole 64-byte line
  of the Ac or Bc block with \texttt{cbo.zero}, inside the pack timer. It covers both packers and is AXPY only.
  Checked on the board: the instruction runs in user mode and the packed data is unchanged.
  \item \textbf{Faster B3 at the shipped nc=128, on both boards} (uninstrumented): \good{+23\%} on \texttt{jbpi}
  (5.52$\to$6.79 GOPs by mean time) and \good{+9\%} on \texttt{jbpi2} (5.49$\to$5.99). Every ``on'' launch beat
  every ``off'' launch on both boards. B3 at nc=384, B3\_K256 and B2 do not change. R24 is mixed (+5\% on
  \texttt{jbpi2}, $-$3\% inside the spread on \texttt{jbpi}).
  \item \textbf{But not by removing reads.} On both boards the pack's L2 loads do \up{not fall} (at nc=128 they
  \up{rise by $\sim$40\%}), and its L2 stores \up{rise by about one per line cleared}. \texttt{cbo.zero} on the X60
  seems to write the zero line out to the L2 rather than just claim it in the L1.
  \item \textbf{The instrumented build goes the other way} on both boards: B3\_nc128 drops from about 8.1 GOPs (no
  slow reps) to about 5.7 (about 60\% slow reps). The switch seems to move B3\_nc128 between its fast and slow
  states, and the timers change which way it goes.
  \item \textbf{Decision so far: not shipped on by default.} Next: the same loop over the lines \emph{without}
  \texttt{cbo.zero}, to separate ``clearing helps'' from ``a short delay before the pack helps''.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Checks.} Host: the line finder on ranges starting and ending on and off line edges; the real generated

\paragraph{How to read it.} The spec expected the pack's L2 loads to \emph{fall} by about one per line cleared
(\iss{42}'s probe saw about one L2 load per line in a pure write stream). On both boards they do not fall, and the L2
stores \emph{rise} by about one per line cleared, in every case. The simplest reading is that the X60 carries out
\texttt{cbo.zero} as a write of a zero line to the L2. At nc=128, where Bc is packed three times per rep, the packer
then misses the L1 on those lines and reads them back (L1D misses and L2 loads both up). A rough estimate, not a
measurement: with the switch off, the pack's L2 loads for B3 at nc=384 are only about 2.8e3 above the lines the
packer reads from A and B ($\approx$1.08e4), while a read of every line written would add about 1.09e4. So inside the
GEMM there may have been little ``read before write'' traffic to remove in the first place.

\textbf{Next:} (1) a control that runs the same loop over the lines \emph{without} \texttt{cbo.zero} (same time
spent, nothing cleared). If B3\_nc128 speeds up just as much, the gain comes from timing, not clearing. (2) Put the
result in \iss{41}. (3) \iss{44} (pack B one column at a time) stays specified and on hold. The switch stays off by
default; B3's nc=128 override remains the baseline.
