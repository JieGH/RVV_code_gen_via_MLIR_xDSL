01 · Packing impact

Scalar pack vs. vector pack, head-to-head

Same three facets — time, share of total kernel time, and memory bandwidth — but now scalar and vector sit side by side per case so the win (or loss) reads directly off each pair, instead of across two separate cards.

Which buffer is being packed? Both. Every BLIS-style GEMM here packs two operands per tile before the microkernel runs: Pack A copies an Mc×Kc panel of matrix A into the contiguous scratch buffer Ac, and Pack B copies a Kc×Nc panel of matrix B into Bc. driver_benchmark.cpp times them as two separate chrono windows per tile (Pack B first, per jc step; Pack A second, per ic step) but adds both into the same pack_time/pack_bytes accumulators — so every "scalar" / "vector" bar below, and everywhere else in this report, is Pack A + Pack B combined, not one or the other. This run's logs don't carry a per-buffer split; getting one would mean adding separate accumulators in the driver.

02 · GEMM compute performance

Compute-kernel throughput, hardware counters, and run-to-run jitter

Isolating the microkernel compute region (packing excluded): achieved GOPs (≈ GFLOP/s for this fp32 kernel), the five hardware counters captured per issue #8, and how much a case's GOPs wobbles across its timed sweep.

Compute throughput (GOPs)

Median GOPs over the timed sweep, by case.

What it is: billions of floating-point operations the compute region alone sustains per second — packing time is excluded, so this is the microkernel's realized arithmetic rate, not the whole kernel's.

What we see: nine of the ten cases sit in a 7.7–9.5 GOPs band regardless of shape or family — B1, B4, B5, S1–S5 cluster tighter still at 7.7–8.7 GOPs, with B3 sitting a bit above that cluster at 9.5 because of the jitter noted below. B2 breaks the pattern at 13.0 GOPs, roughly 60% above the rest of the pack — the counters below explain why. The three new large-Square cases, S3 (3000³), S4 (4000³), and S5 (5000³), land right inside that same 7.7–8.0 GOPs cluster as S1/S2 and B4 — the kernel's throughput doesn't fall off as the problem grows 5× larger.

Hardware performance counters — compute region

Instructions/cycle, arithmetic throughput, and L1D/backend behavior while the microkernel is running.

IPCinstructions retired / cycle
How much useful work the core finishes each clock tick. Ceiling is set by the pipeline's issue width; anything well below it means cycles are going by with nothing retiring — the core is waiting, not computing.
FLOPs / cyclearithmetic throughput
Floating-point operations completed per cycle — IPC narrowed to just the vector FMA lanes. It's the number that turns cycles into GOPs; a kernel can have decent IPC from address math and loop overhead while this stays low.
L1D read/write miss %cache locality
Share of load/store accesses that miss the L1 data cache and go further out for data. This is the counter that judges whether packing did its job — Ac/Bc should be small and contiguous enough to stay resident through the whole microkernel.
Stall (backend) %cycles with nothing to retire
Fraction of cycles where the backend has no instruction ready to issue. High stall with low cache misses (see below) points at a different bottleneck — instruction latency or dependency chains — not memory.
Jitter / CV%run-to-run stability
Coefficient of variation of GOPs across the sweep (std÷median). Low CV means the median is a trustworthy single number; high CV means the measurement itself is unstable and any one rep could mislead. See the chart below.
What the numbers say together: across all ten cases, L1D read-miss stays under 3% and write-miss under 3.5% — packing is doing its job, the data the microkernel touches is resident. Yet the backend still stalls 44–54% of cycles for nine of the ten cases, and IPC (0.53–0.73) and FLOPs/cycle (4.75–5.79) sit well below what the vector unit could sustain. Low misses plus high stalls rules out memory as the bottleneck there — the ceiling is more likely instruction-level: FMA latency and dependency chains between accumulator registers not being hidden by enough independent work in flight.

B2 is still the exception on every one of these axes at once: highest IPC (0.97), highest FLOPs/cycle (7.70), lowest L1D read-miss (0.4%), lowest stall (28.1%). Its whole working set (384×384×64) is small enough to stay pinned in cache for the entire sweep, so the backend rarely has to wait — which is also exactly why it's the one case that broke out of the throughput band above. S3–S5 track S1/S2 almost exactly on every counter (IPC 0.53–0.54, stall 52.7–54.1%), so this instruction-bound ceiling holds uniformly as the Square shapes scale from 1000³ to 5000³.

Run-to-run jitter

Whisker = min–max across the sweep; band = P5–P95; dot = median. CV% labeled above each case.

What it means: every case ran as a sweep of repeated timings, not one measurement. The whisker shows the full min–max spread, the band shows the middle 90% (P5–P95), and CV% (std÷median) is the single-number summary — low CV means the reps agree with each other, high CV means the timing is bimodal or noisy and no single rep should be trusted alone.

What we see: nine cases hold CV ≤2% — tight, reliable measurements, including all three new large-Square cases (S3–S5, CV 0%). B3 is again the outlier, CV 9% (min 8.6 vs. max 12.5 GOPs) — slightly tighter than the prior run's 11%, but still the same shape flagged since issue #1 as bimodally unstable. B3's M=64 with MR=16 means only 4 microkernel calls reuse each packed block, so there's little chance for the packed data to "settle" in cache before the block is discarded — run-to-run cache state varies more than it does for higher-reuse shapes.
03 · Overall vs. the reference design and vs. yesterday

BERT gains ground on the reference; Square's gap repeats almost exactly

Issue #2 (closed) recorded a prior design's GOPs for seven of these shapes/tiles — RVV compute with scalar packing, user-supplied at the time. This run swaps in RVV vector packing; the chart below is this run's compute GOPs against that reference. The three new Square cases (S3–S5) have no such reference yet, since issue #2 never benchmarked shapes past 2000³.

This run vs. reference design

This run (vector pack) Reference design (issue #2, scalar pack)
Reading the comparison, and how it moved since yesterday's run (2026-09-16_221606):
  • BERT leads the reference by more than it did yesterday — B1 +8.3% (was +1.0%), B3 +8.0% (was +5.5%), B5 +9.7% (was +4.0%) all gained several points day-over-day. B2 holds essentially flat at +4.0% (was +5.1%).
  • B4 still trails the reference, and by almost the same margin as yesterday: −6.8% (was −6.6%) — a consistent, repeatable gap rather than noise.
  • Both Square shapes still trail, and the gap barely moved: S1 −2.9% (was −2.1%), S2 −10.1% (was −10.0%). S2's ~10% shortfall now shows up in two independent runs a day apart, which argues against issue #7's "N_REPS=5 sampling noise" explanation and toward a real, repeatable gap for larger square shapes that's worth a closer look.
  • Net: +1.4% average across the 7 cases with a reference — up from −0.4% yesterday, driven almost entirely by BERT's gains; Square remains flat-to-worse in both runs.
  • New this run: the Square sweep now extends to S3 (3000³), S4 (4000³), S5 (5000³) with no external reference yet, but their throughput (7.73–7.95 GOPs) sits in the same band as S1/S2 — whatever is holding Square back relative to the reference applies uniformly across problem size, not just at S1/S2's scale.

What we see: throughput barely moves across a 125× increase in problem volume (1000³ to 5000³) — S1 8.25, S2 7.82, S3 7.95, S4 7.73, S5 7.84 GOPs, all within a 0.5 GOPs band. Where a reference exists, this run trails it by about the same margin at both sizes tested (S1 −2.9%, S2 −10.1%), and S3–S5 sit in that same 7.7–8.0 GOPs band with no reference to compare against yet — so whatever caps Square below the reference is a property of the Square shape/tile, not something that gets worse (or better) as the cube grows.
